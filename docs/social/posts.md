## LinkedIn

Attach the same video. LinkedIn moves links lower in reach, so the post works as a story with the links near the end.

```
When I was looking for a good 3D anatomy explorer, almost everything I found was either paid, locked behind an app store, or just pre-rendered pictures on a website. I wanted something anyone could open in a browser tab and actually explore. 🫀

So I built it. Meet Body Atlas 3D.

It's a full human-body explorer that runs right in your browser, on desktop or phone:

🦴 2,234 real structures across 15 systems: bones, muscles, vessels, nerves and organs, each one clickable
👫 Male and female reference bodies, with the same systems in both
💥 Exploded view: pull the whole body apart, or just one group, like the skull's 22 bones
🏷️ Labels pinned to the body with leader lines, and a slider to fade any system
🫀 14 organs that lift out as detailed models. The heart beats and the lungs breathe while you read
🎯 A "Find the …" quiz to test yourself

A few things I'm proud of:
• It's built entirely on open anatomy data (BodyParts3D and the HuBMAP female reference set), credited and licensed CC BY 4.0
• 2.3 million triangles draw in about 15 GPU calls, so it stays smooth even on modest laptops
• It installs as an app and works offline after the first visit, and every system has its own readable page

Explore it: https://body-atlas-3d.imlistack.workers.dev
Code (open source): https://github.com/ImliStack/Body-Atlas-3D

If you find it useful, I'd love two small things 🙏
🤝 Share it with a student, teacher or anyone curious about how the body fits together
⭐ Give the project a star on GitHub. It really helps open-source work get seen

It's a learning tool, not a clinical one. What should I add next: cross-sections, guided tours, or something else? Tell me in the comments 👇

Built under ImliStack — https://imlistack.com

#Anatomy #3D #ThreeJS #WebGL #OpenSource #MedicalEducation #BuildInPublic #ReactJS
```
